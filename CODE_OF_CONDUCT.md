## Expected Conduct

- Be precise about what is known, inferred, blocked, historical, locally tested,
  or live-proven.
- Preserve other contributors' work, ownership boundaries, and active worktrees.
- Prefer evidence, reproducible steps, and explicit gaps over confident claims.
- Treat accessibility, compact interaction design, mobile constraints, and safe
  failure states as product requirements.
- Respect user data, credentials, profiles, sessions, proxies, screenshots, and
  operational context as sensitive by default.
- Stop or escalate when an action is destructive, irreversible, credential-gated,
  externally productive, or outside the granted scope.

## Unacceptable Conduct

- Hiding failures, fabricating evidence, or declaring work complete from an
  unverified selector, mock, screenshot, or transcript.
- Exposing credentials, tokens, cookies, raw proxy URLs, private browser state,
  or recovery material in code, logs, prompts, screenshots, or reports.
- Overwriting unrelated changes, silently changing acceptance criteria, or using
  another agent's worktree without coordination.
- Giving a harness broader access than its identity, lease, ticket, or approval
  permits.
- Treating humans or agents disrespectfully, or using automation to bypass
  consent, authorization, platform rules, or project governance.

## Review and Enforcement

Concerns should be reported to the project owner with the smallest useful,
redacted evidence. The owner may pause work, revoke an agent or harness lease,
invalidate evidence, require rework, or reject a contribution. Security concerns
follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md); technical disagreements follow
[GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).
